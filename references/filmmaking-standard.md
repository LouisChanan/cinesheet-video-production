# Filmmaking standard — CineSheet reference

**Source boundary**: summarized from the user's previously provided *Think Like a Movie Maker* New Wine International Church course (37 pages). Detailed Session 1: imagery/camera (pp. 2–20) and Session 2: audio (pp. 21–37). The course roadmap also names Sessions 3–5 but the provided PDF lacks their detailed lesson content. The process sections below are CineSheet **applications**, not quotations or mandates from the slides.

## Shot size — choose for story purpose

| Size | Audience question | Typical purpose |
|---|---|---|
| EWS Extreme Wide | Where are we? | Establish scale and landscape |
| WS Wide | Who and where? | Blocking, body action, relation to environment |
| MS Medium | What are they doing? | Interaction and visible gestures |
| CU Close-up | How do they feel? | Emotional reaction or key object |
| ECU Extreme Close-up | Which detail matters? | Crucial tiny detail or evidence |

## Angle/perspective

- Eye Level: human-scale natural interaction; OTS: relation between speakers; POV: show viewer what character sees.
- Dutch Angle: instability/confusion, not decoration. Low Angle: perceived authority/power; High Angle: vulnerability or reduced status.

## Movement — physical distinction matters

- **Tilt/Pan**: rotate camera from a fixed position vertically/horizontally.
- **Dolly In/Out**: move whole camera toward/away, emphasizing or revealing context.
- **Truck/Track**: camera physically moves laterally. **Follow**: camera follows moving subject.
- **Static** is allowed as a CineSheet practical extension, not listed as a course movement lesson.

## Four sound layers

1. Dialogue — words and comprehensibility.
2. Ambience — believable place and room tone.
3. Sound FX (SFX) — precise action impacts, incl. Foley.
4. Background Music (BGM) — tonal/musical emotional guidance, *under* important speech and effects.

**Course nuance:** Sound is described as at least 50% of film-viewing experience as a teaching emphasis, not a measured universal statistic. Sound should be natural, clear and synchronized. Course dialogue guidance: record close, in quiet place, test, enunciate, and capture about 10 seconds of room tone. Use music sparingly and with appropriate rights.

## CineSheet-derived per-scene planning checklist (not a verbatim source requirement)

Story beat; audience information; emotion; shot size + why; angle + why; movement + why; action/reaction; four sound layers; timing; continuity. Scene prompt must use only techniques that serve the intended emotion and narrative clarity.
